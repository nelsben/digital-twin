## Why

After the card is filled, a consultant drives the rest with Claude: level the card, understand blockers and warnings, record overrides, regenerate, then build each story of the generated backlog and keep its status. These are the last two skills of the workflow, and the kit's authoring guide requires evals before the skill text. The persona card is card.json (not the persona voice file in starter-kit/templates/persona, and not a task card in agents/tasks); the backlog here is the generated per-customer backlog.json, not this repo's GitHub issues.

## Done when

- Read first, whole: starter-kit/docs/authoring-skills.md, starter-kit/templates/skill/ (README, SKILL.md, both eval files), partner-kit/docs/design.md, partner-kit/plugins/twin-consultant/README.md, the two skills already under partner-kit/plugins/twin-consultant/skills/ (follow their layout, tone and the way they refer to cli.py and to files in the plugin), tests/test_partner_kit_skills.py, data/rules.json, data/plan-fit.json, data/task-library.json, and both fixtures' persona folders under engagements/ (card.json, levelled.json, backlog.json, backlog.md, dashboard.html).

- Both skills live at partner-kit/plugins/twin-consultant/skills/<name>/ with SKILL.md, evals/evals.json, evals/trigger-evals.json, evals/files/ and references/ only where needed. Frontmatter holds name and description only; name equals the folder name; description under 200 characters, third person, says when to use it; body under 150 lines; plain language; none of the words tests/test_interview_kit.py bans in spoken text appear in SKILL.md. Each names the persona card term collision once.
- planning-a-twin. Input: a persona folder whose card validates (if it does not, it says so and points to the filling-persona-cards skill; it does not fix the card itself). It runs the all command, then reads levelled.json and reports in this order: (1) blockers, each with its code, the task or 'persona' it is about, what it means in one plain sentence, the compendium page cited, and the ways out the design allows (change an answer on the card or profile if the answer was wrong, change the task, move the customer to the plan or org the blocker names, or record an override where the design allows one for that decision); (2) decisions that rest on an unverified fact, with what must be confirmed and with whom; (3) decisions left undecided (code decision-undecided), which a person must decide by override; (4) warnings, grouped by code; (5) the card's open questions with owner and due date. It never edits levelled.json, backlog.json, backlog.md or dashboard.html by hand: it changes the card or the profile and runs the command again.
- planning-a-twin and overrides. It records an override only in the card's overrides list, in the shape design section 4.2 gives, and only when the consultant supplies the value, the reason and the named person at the customer who approved it; without all three it asks and writes nothing. It states the rule's own result next to the override and says the dashboard will show the decision as overridden. It never proposes an override on its own to make a blocker disappear, and it never raises an autonomy level above the rule's result unless the consultant asks for exactly that and names the approver. After any change it reruns all and reports what changed between the two runs (decisions, blockers, warnings, backlog stories added or retired).
- planning-a-twin ends with: the path of dashboard.html, the counts of blockers, warnings and open questions, whether the plan is complete, and the next step (the building-twin-stories skill; do not start building here). It also says the backlog can be exported for a tracker with the backlog command's --plan-out option, and does not import it anywhere itself.
- building-twin-stories. Input: a persona folder with backlog.json that is not stale (if the backlog command says levelled.json or the backlog is out of date, it says so and points to planning-a-twin). It picks the next story by the order the backlog gives: stories in the pilot first, then by priority, skipping any story whose gated_by items are not done or that a blocker is about; it shows the story statement, its fields, its acceptance criteria and its tasks, and asks the consultant to confirm the pick or name another story.
- building-twin-stories works one task at a time: it reads the task's how steps and done_when, opens the starter-kit template, runbook or compendium page each step links, and does the work with the consultant in the customer's delivery repo using those templates (it does not restate them). The kit's rule holds: eval cases are written before the skill text, and a task that needs real examples from the skill owner waits for them and invents none. A step that only a customer admin can do (admin console, connector approval, identity provider, device management) is not done by Claude: the skill hands over the runbook link and the exact values from levelled.json, and leaves the task in-progress until the consultant says the admin has done it.
- building-twin-stories and status. It changes only status values in backlog.json, only where design section 8 says a person sets them, and only todo to in-progress to done; it marks a task done only when its done_when holds and the consultant confirms. After a status change it runs the backlog and dashboard commands so backlog.md and dashboard.html agree, and never edits those two by hand. It builds each story at the autonomy start level in levelled.json, not the ceiling. It never puts a real customer's names or data in a sample-* folder. It ends each session with: what was done, what is in-progress and who it waits on, and the next story.
- Evals for planning-a-twin: at least five cases in evals.json (the Team fixture: report warnings grouped, no blocker; the private-equity fixture: the two undecided autonomy ceilings and the unverified items are reported and nothing is filled in; a card changed as the expectations variant team-c4-gmail-send does, so a blocker is explained with its ways out; an override request with no approver named, expected to write nothing and ask; a should-not-trigger near miss) and at least eight trigger queries, half should_trigger false, the negatives being near misses (filling the card from notes, writing the persona voice file, this repo's own task cards, building a story). Evals for building-twin-stories: at least five cases (pick the next story on the Team fixture and name its first task; a story gated by an unfinished item is skipped; an admin-only task is handed over and left in-progress; a request to mark a task done whose done_when is not met is declined; a should-not-trigger near miss) and at least eight trigger queries, half false. Expectations are checkable statements about files or about what the reply names, taken from the fixtures' real values (story ids, task ids, codes).
