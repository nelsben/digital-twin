## Persona answers

### P1. Your role

"I'm an associate on the deal team, one of 18 of us across partners, principals and associates. I work on an Intune laptop that IT manages, and for anything on a live deal I'm in Claude Code on the firm's sign-in. Nothing deal-specific goes in chat."

### P2. Who you serve

"The deal team. I write for the principal on the deal first, and the partners read it after them."

### P3. A good week

"A good week is one where the briefs and screening notes are done by Wednesday, so I can spend the rest on the model and the diligence calls. The jobs I'd hand over: company briefs, the CRM update after a meeting, call prep and first-pass CIM screening, and the memo drafting the partners ask for."

### P4. How it should sound

"Like a careful associate. Short sentences, every number with where it came from, no adjectives. If something is uncertain, say why."

### P5. Never, and ask first

"Never pull anything from the investor-relations side. Never search the web on a live deal; the query alone gives away what we're looking at."

### P6. Who you turn to

"The principal on the deal. Anything about the wall or MNPI goes to the Chief Compliance Officer."

## Task block

Task id: T1    Task name: Prepare a one-page company brief

#### F1. Your tasks

"About four a week, around 90 minutes each. About three of us on the team do it. It's the biggest single chunk of my week. It's the first thing I'd want in the pilot. I want it to draft the one-page company brief from Salesforce and SharePoint, so I spend my time on the model and the diligence calls instead."

#### F2. What goes in, what comes out

"The deal record in Salesforce, then the prior memos and data-room files in SharePoint; I only look at those. Out comes one page for the deal team. It's all deal material, so highly sensitive."

#### F3. What good looks like

"I'll share two past briefs, one good and one weak. Not collected yet. Callum Nwosu is who I'd ask whether a brief is right."

#### F4. If it goes wrong

"Medium. A wrong fact can reach a partner discussion. A reviewer checks every brief before it goes further."

#### F5. What starts it

"I do. A meeting is booked and I start the brief."

#### F6. Who else sees it

"The deal team, and it may feed a partner discussion. Nobody outside the firm."

#### F7. Undo and money

"It's a draft; I can throw it away. It doesn't touch money."

#### F8. Things written by outsiders

"No. Data-room files are the target's documents, but they sit in our SharePoint and we've already reviewed what goes in."

#### F9. Remembering

"I don't want it to remember things between briefs. Each one should start fresh from Salesforce and SharePoint. Nothing about one deal should be remembered into another."

## Task block

Task id: T4    Task name: Screen an inbound CIM or teaser

#### F1. Your tasks

"About five a week, 40 minutes each. Not for the pilot; after briefs and CRM. I want it to screen the CIM or teaser and suggest pursue or pass, so the screening notes are done by Wednesday."

#### F2. What goes in, what comes out

"The document I upload, or one in SharePoint. If it came by email I save it to SharePoint first. Then Salesforce for whether we've spoken to the company before. I only look at all of that. Out comes a screening summary with a pursue-or-pass suggestion. It's deal material, so highly sensitive."

#### F3. What good looks like

"No examples yet for this one. Beatriz Halloran is who I'd ask whether a screening is right."

#### F4. If it goes wrong

"Medium to high. A missed red flag, or a good deal passed over. Call it high."

#### F5. What starts it

"Really it's a new document arriving, but for now I'd start it myself."

#### F6. Who else sees it

"The principal who decides whether we pursue. It stays inside the firm."

#### F7. Undo and money

"It's a draft for the principal. No money."

#### F8. Things written by outsiders

"No. The CIM is the seller's document, but it's saved to our SharePoint and we read it as a document, not as instructions."

#### F9. Remembering

"No memory."
